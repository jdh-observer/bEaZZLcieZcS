1/ New in @Journal_DigHist: Can transformer language models do useful work for intellectual historians? We tested one on 160,000 documents from the American Founders. Here's what it found — and what it missed. 🧵

2/ "Republican" underwent a measurable functional shift between the Founding era and the Early National period: from a description of governmental form to a marker of partisan identity. That shift is now visible across the full corpus, not just the texts historians chose to read.

3/ "Liberty," by contrast, is among the most *stable* words in the corpus. Its contestation didn't work through the word shifting meaning — it worked through a stable term applied to radically unequal people. Measurement helps establish which kind of historical argument you have.

4/ The workflow trains one GPT-2 model on the historical corpus (no modern pretraining), then cross-validates results across three instruments. Where they agree, the signal is in the corpus. Where they disagree, close reading resolves what measurement can't.

5/ Under a rigorous multiple-testing correction, no term's change is statistically significant. We report that plainly. A method that resists false findings is more useful than one that manufactures them. The near-null result is itself informative.

6/ Every step is documented in open, runnable code. The trained Founders language model is on Hugging Face for researchers working on early American history or related corpora: https://huggingface.co/egweinbe/founders-gpt2
